## **Government / Political Structure Terms**

- **Monarchy** — A single ruler (king/queen/emperor) holds governing power, usually inherited.
- **Constitutional Monarchy** — Monarch exists, but actual power is limited by law or parliament.
- **Republic** — Leaders are elected; no hereditary ruler.
- **Council Government** — A group of officials collectively make high-level decisions.
- **Parliament** — A legislative body that creates and debates laws.
- **Oligarchy** — Power held by a small elite group (wealthy or powerful families).
- **Dictatorship** — One leader with total unchecked power; opposition is suppressed.
- **Autocracy** — Broad term for government controlled by a single authority.
- **Technocracy** — Officials chosen for expertise rather than elections or lineage.
- **Bureaucracy** — Administrative systems run by government employees; slow but structured.
- **Ministry / Department** — Branches of government responsible for specific sectors (e.g., health, defense).
- **Civil Service** — Non-political workers who run daily government operations.
- **State Propaganda** — Government-managed messaging that shapes public opinion.
- **Secret Police** — Covert force used to monitor or intimidate the population.
- **Martial Law** — Military takes over civilian government during emergencies.

---

## **Legal / Power Terms**

- **Human Rights Violations** — Government actions that deny basic freedoms.
- **Censorship** — Blocking or controlling information, media, and speech.
- **Corruption** — Abuse of power for personal gain (bribes, favoritism).
- **Sanctions** — Economic/political penalties imposed by other countries.
- **Treason** — Crimes against the ruling state (real or fabricated).

---

## **Economy Terms**

- **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** — Total value of everything a country produces; a measure of economic strength.
- **Exports / Imports** — Goods sent out of / brought into a country.
- **Trade Embargo** — Ban on trade with a specific country.
- **Inflation** — Prices rise, money loses value.
- **Black Market** — Illegal trading when goods are restricted or expensive.
- **Agrarian Economy** — Farming is the main source of income.
- **Industrial Economy** — Factories and manufactured goods drive the economy.
- **Service Economy** — Jobs like transport, healthcare, education dominate.
- **Subsidies** — Government pays industries or groups to keep prices low.
- **Monopoly** — One company controls an entire industry.
- **Consumer Safety Regulations** — Laws to protect buyers (can be absent or corrupt).
- **Cost of Living** — How expensive it is to survive day-to-day.

---

## **Military / Control Terms**

- **Conscription** — Mandatory military service.
- **Checkpoints** — Government control over movement within borders.
- **Surveillance State** — Government constantly monitors citizens (cameras, data).
- **Militia** — Local armed groups, unofficial but tolerated or encouraged.

---

## **Society & Class Structure Terms**

- **Social Stratification** — Clear social classes (rich vs poor).
- **Caste or Rank System** — Fixed roles based on birth or status.
- **Nationalism** — Strong belief in national superiority; can be weaponized.
- **Ethnic Tension** — Conflicts between identity groups within the state.


## **Economic System Labels**
*(How a country decides who owns what and who gets what)*

**Capitalism**
Private individuals/companies own most businesses; competition drives the economy.

**State Capitalism**
Government owns major industries but still operates them for profit like corporations.

**Socialism**
Major resources and industries are owned or heavily regulated by the state for public welfare. Wealth distribution is more equalized.

**Communism**
No private property officially; everything owned collectively through the state. In practice: heavy central control.

**Mixed Economy**
Blend of private business and government control. Most modern nations fall here.

**Command Economy**
Government decides what gets made, in what quantity, and at what price.

**Market Economy**
Supply and demand rule — prices, production, and profit driven by consumers and competition.

**Subsistence Economy**
People produce mainly just enough to survive (common in rural or underdeveloped regions).

**Informal Economy**
Work done outside government oversight — street vendors, unregistered labor, black markets.



---

## **Structural Economic Profiles**
*(What the country actually does to make money)*

**Agrarian Economy**
Farming and livestock are the backbone.

**Industrial Economy**
Factories, manufacturing, machinery — smokestacks everywhere.

**Resource-Dependent Economy**
Wealth comes from extracting resources like oil, minerals, or timber.

**Service-Based Economy**
Tourism, healthcare, transport, education, finance — selling skills rather than products.

**Post-Industrial Economy**
High tech, R&D, automation; manufacturing becomes secondary.



---

## **Development Status Terms**

*(How stable and sustainable the system is)*

**Developed Economy**
High income, strong infrastructure, long life expectancy.

**Developing Economy**
Growing but uneven; urban centers advance faster than rural areas.

**Fragile / Failing Economy**
Corruption, instability, shortages — survival is the daily task.

**Isolated Economy**
Limited trade, protectionist policies, distrust of outsiders.
